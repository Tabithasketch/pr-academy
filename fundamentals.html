Lesson 1: Introduction to Public Relations

Public Relations is a strategic communication process that builds and maintains mutually beneficial relationships between an organisation and its publics.

What is the purpose of PR?

  • Building trust and understanding.
  • Maintaining a positive reputation.
  • Communicating with different publics.
  • Managing relationships between organisations and people.

Important PR terms

Publics: Groups of people who have an interest in or are affected by an organisation.

Reputation: The way an organisation is perceived by others.

Communication: The exchange of information and ideas.

Lesson 2: History of Public Relations

Public Relations developed over time as individuals, organisations and governments recognised the importance of communicating with the public.

Important historical figures

Ivy Lee: Known for promoting openness and accurate information in organisational communication.

Edward Bernays: A major figure in the development of modern Public Relations and the use of research and persuasion.

How PR has evolved

  • Early publicity and public information.
  • Professional communication and media relations.
  • Research-based strategic PR.
  • Digital and social media communication.

Lesson 3: Functions of Public Relations

PR practitioners perform several important functions to help organisations build relationships with their publics.

  • Media relations: Maintaining relationships with journalists and media organisations.
  • Publicity: Generating attention and media coverage.
  • Crisis communication: Communicating during difficult situations.
  • Community relations: Building relationships with local communities.
  • Internal communication: Communicating with employees.
  • Research: Understanding public opinions and needs.
